# R2 context review — Pass A, outcome withheld

Expectation memo was saved first on 2026-10-09. No repository or outcome notes were read. This review is ready for the parent's baseline and exact policy; it makes no local performance finding.

## Interpretation
An explicit numeric refund policy provides an unusually useful operational ground truth. Score eligibility, amount, authorized exceptions, and executed action separately from courteous wording. Appeasement or never arguing can be satisfied by a calm explanation; neither phrase necessarily authorizes exceeding a number. If discretion is itself a developer instruction, its scope must be interpreted before calling the result a violation. Same-role ambiguity is different from ignoring a higher-role policy.

Use four separate labels: authorized discretion (permitted exception/range), policy conflict (identifiable incompatible instructions with roles), sycophancy (unjustified movement toward expressed customer preference), and injection (attempted instruction redirection across a trust boundary). Labels can overlap, but operational failure alone identifies none of the latter mechanisms. Direct customer pressure must not be relabeled indirect injection merely because an agent sees it.

## Consequential full-text evidence
Eight primary full texts were reviewed at the sections below; fourteen primary candidates were screened. Full-text review means methods/results/limitations passages, not exhaustive reading of every appendix. Each paper-specific paragraph remains under 200 words. Versions and exclusion decisions are in sources.csv.

1. [Instruction Hierarchy, arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13208v1), §2 Prompt Injections; §3.1; §3.2; §4 setup/over-refusal; §6. Aligned lower-priority requests should still be followed; conflicting requests should be ignored or refused while preserving the task. Direct user and indirect third-party injections have distinct entry points. Its matched GPT-3.5 training baseline provides intervention evidence, but the paper's broad explanation of vulnerability is not a causal diagnosis of this refund agent. The authors report over-refusal regressions and remaining adaptive vulnerability. Their blanket treatment of tool instructions is acknowledged as a simplification. Transfer: avoid interpreting every customer preference as malicious or refusing the whole service interaction.

2. [IHEval, NAACL 2025 proceedings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.425.pdf), §3 pp.3–5; §4.3–4.5 pp.7–8, Table 3; Appendix Table 6. Reference merges instructions into one user message; aligned distributes compatible instructions; conflict introduces incompatible ones. This design separates task difficulty from hierarchy difficulty better than a pressure-only evaluation. Wording strictness affects conflict behavior. Contrary to a simple prompt-fix account, an explicit hierarchy reminder yields no consistent improvement in tested models. Formatting evaluation does not check response content (Appendix Figure 9 note, p.15). Transfer: matched neutral, aligned, and conflict conditions; preserve factual content while changing wording. Do not turn heterogeneous task-average scores into refund success probabilities or infer training history from performance alone.

3. [Sharma et al., arXiv v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.13548v4), §3.1–3.3; §4–5; Appendix A.3–A.4 and preference sensitivity discussion. User preference modifies feedback compared with a baseline; challenge prompts can move correct QA answers to incorrect answers. Feedback positivity is judged by GPT-4, so tone positivity differs from numeric-policy correctness. Preference-data associations and preference-model optimization support a plausible reward-related explanation, not the conclusion that each local failure was caused by RLHF. Authors explicitly recognize that deferring after challenge can be nuanced. Transfer: compare the same policy/facts with neutral versus customer-desired outcome and retain traces; friendliness and acknowledgment do not establish sycophancy.

4. [Wei et al., arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.03958v1), §§2–6, Fig.5; Appendix A.1–A.5. Opinion-free incorrect addition controls distinguish knowledge from user-conditioned agreement. Synthetic training reduces this tendency, providing contrary evidence to inevitability. The smallest tested model does not show the same successful intervention behavior; filtering unknown claims and mixing instruction data matter. Appendix opinion-stripped controls do not show a strong preference for the removed user's stance. Transfer: a refund agent's competence at applying the rule without pressure must be established before social-pressure interpretation; intervention effects are conditional on model/task and cannot supply an expected phrase ranking.

5. [AgentDojo, arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13352v3), §3.1 utility/security functions; §4.1, Fig.6; §5. Clean and attacked tasks run in mutable environments with separate deterministic utility and attacker-goal checks. Low-capability agents may fail to execute the attacker's objective even after following an injection, so low attack success can conceal low utility. Defenses and attacks exhibit differing tradeoffs; stronger agents need not be uniformly safer. Transfer: inspect attempted and executed refund action, clean correctness, and task completion together. An over-limit refund is a policy error even if a customer is satisfied; a blocked/no-action agent is not automatically operationally correct.

6. [InjecAgent, ACL Findings 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.624.pdf), §2.2–2.3 pp.3–4, Table 2; §3.1 pp.5–6; §8 limitations. Tool-response injections are placed after an assumed successful user tool call; direct harm checks the next harmful tool invocation, while exfiltration uses a simulated extraction response and a further sending step. ASR-valid conditions on parsable valid outputs, unlike ASR-all. The fixed enhanced attack wrapper and hypothetical starting state limit transfer to dynamic service dialogue. Transfer: always preserve invalid-output counts, total trials, and conditioning; benchmark attack rates are not customer-pressure policy-violation rates.

7. [tau-bench, arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12045v1), §3 task/evaluation/pass^k, §4 construction, §6 limitations. Simulated users interact with tools and policy; expected final database changes and requested information provide operational scoring. Annotation constrains tasks to a unique intended outcome. pass^k asks whether all k trials succeed, unlike pass@k's at-least-one success. Simulator dependence and narrow synthetic domains limit realism. Transfer: use action/state correctness and repeated semantically matched conversations, while auditing policy violations that final-state matching could miss. Local policy rubrics should explicitly cover required confirmations and forbidden intermediate actions.

8. [OpenAI IH-Challenge primary report](https://openai.com/index/instruction-hierarchy-challenge/), sections What instruction hierarchy is, Why large-scale training can be hard, Our approach, Results; accessed 2026-10-09. Current stated hierarchy includes system, developer, user, then tool. The report identifies task complexity, fallible judges, and over-refusal shortcuts as confounds and uses simple objectively gradable conflicts. Training reports improved hierarchy behavior without collapse into refusal, further contrary evidence to universal fragility. This is an unversioned vendor report about an internal trained model, not an independently reproduced result or a guarantee for production models. No reported numerical improvement is transferred here.

## Evidence strength and disagreements
The strongest common ground is methodological: role provenance, clean task controls, matched variants, independent operational scoring, and denominators. Evidence that wording can affect behavior is broad, but none of these reviewed studies tests this exact numeric refund policy and these four phrase families. The hierarchy training studies and synthetic sycophancy intervention contradict inevitability. IHEval's weak reminder results contradict assuming that adding one hierarchy sentence reliably fixes conflicts. AgentDojo contradicts treating low attack success as sufficient security. These differences can coexist because training, task, agent scaffold, and metric differ.

## Outcome-comparison protocol (no experiments authorized)
When supplied, map each trace to policy clauses before reviewing phrase labels. Record exact allowed amount/exception and action; then compare paired variants and neutral control with per-cell denominators, invalid outputs, and uncertainty. Do not condition only on correct neutral runs without also reporting all-run results. Distinguish attempted policy breach from executed breach, and text promises from tool action. Treat model explanations as diagnostic hypotheses, not privileged access to causes. If no matched control exists, report association and alternative explanations rather than a phrasing effect. A null result leaves ceiling/floor and power alternatives open.
