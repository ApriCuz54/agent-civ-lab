# R7 context review — outcome-withheld Pass A

Status: expectation_memo.md frozen before full-text review and before any baseline or repository access. Review date 2026-10-09. Six consequential primary full texts inspected, pinned below. This is a contextual assessment, not an experiment or a prediction fitted to local results.

## Assessment
Independent sampling and answer aggregation already provide a strong mechanism for improving arithmetic accuracy. A mixed-model advantage over repeated samples of one model is credible only if it survives capability, budget, difficulty and aggregation controls. The current evidence supports testing complementary errors; it does not establish that model count or communication independently improves reasoning.

## Full-text evidence cards

**S1 — Wang et al., Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models, arXiv 2203.11171v4.** Sections 2–3, Table 1, especially aggregation equations and task-dependent parser. Multiple sampled reasoning paths are aggregated by final answer; this is a same-model baseline with extra inference, not communication. Unweighted voting and normalized weighted sums were similar in Table 1; other weighting schemes performed worse. Transfer: parsing and vote rules are part of the treatment. Limit: historical models and prompted benchmarks cannot quantify modern mixed-model benefit. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.11171v4).

**S2 — Li et al., More Agents Is All You Need, arXiv 2402.05120v1.** Sections 3–6, Figures 1 and 6–7. Sampling-and-voting scales without requiring debate. Its constructed arithmetic experiment separates operand magnitude, step count and answer-space size; gains peak then decline as inherent difficulty rises. Transfer: stratify the bank by difficulty and avoid collapsing ceiling and impossible items into one mean. Limit: scaling the sample count changes inference budget; apparent small-model/large-model parity is not a diversity-isolated result. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.05120v1).

**S3 — Zhang et al., If Multi-Agent Debate is the Answer, What is the Question?, arXiv 2502.08788v1.** PDF pp. 5–9, Sections 3–4, Tables 2–4. Five debate methods, nine benchmarks and four models are compared with CoT and self-consistency. Methods generally fail to reliably dominate the simpler controls; heterogeneous model outputs help some frameworks. Budget alignment uses roughly six LLM calls, not equal total tokens. Transfer: heterogeneity is promising but increased context and unequal capability remain plausible contributors. Access caveat: HTML omitted the body; the 26-page PDF supplied the full text. [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.08788v1).

**S4 — Choi et al., Debate or Vote, arXiv 2508.17536v1.** Sections 3–4 and 6; Tables 1, 3–4; Appendix F. Initial independent voting often matches or beats later debate; communication topology changes performance. The majority-vote theorem assumes independent homogeneous draws and a correct answer that is uniquely modal. Its martingale conclusion follows the specified Bayesian update, not arbitrary LLM behavior. IMPORTANT: the heterogeneous extension in Section 6 uses personas on the same base model, not distinct model families. Transfer: it supports an independent-vote control but does not resolve the proposed mixed-model contrast. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17536v1).

**S5 — Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems, arXiv 2512.08296v1.** Sections 4.1–4.4, Figure 2, Tables 4–5. Architecture effects across 180 configurations depend on task structure: parallelizable financial analysis gains while sequential planning degrades. Authors report matched reasoning-token budgets and tool access. Transfer: topology and communication overhead belong in budget accounting. Limit: tool-using architectures and synthesis differ materially from fixed arithmetic majority vote; fitted thresholds and error-amplification coefficients should not be imported as universal laws. Budget language also alternates between iterations and reasoning tokens, requiring implementation verification. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296v1).

**S6 — Ferreira et al., Beyond Symmetric Agents, arXiv 2609.35875v1.** Sections 3.1–3.2, 5.1–5.5; Tables 1–2; Appendices B, G, H. Recent preprint pairs debate with independent samples from identical rosters and matching total generations. Mixed-team debate loses to its sampling control on the pooled core comparison; temperature spreading is null and extreme personas often hurt. Total tokens remain higher for debate despite generation matching. Authors correct context overflow and thinking-budget artifacts that otherwise alter conclusions. Transfer: audit effective context and actual reasoning budgets. Limit: small open-weight models, modest fixed banks, overlapping teams and extreme persona construction constrain generalization. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35875v1).

## What the neutral design can establish
A fixed bank and matched mean member accuracy remove some obvious confounding but not taskwise capability. Two models with identical mean accuracy can solve different difficulty slices. Selecting accuracy-matched teams on the evaluation bank creates selection bias and regression-to-the-mean; match on calibration items and show held-out balance with uncertainty. If the bank is too small for a split, characterize matching as descriptive and reserve causal language.

Correctness indicators E_i yield Var(sum E_i)=sum Var(E_i)+2 sum Cov(E_i,E_j). This explains why covariance matters but does not determine plurality success: wrong answers may disperse or collide. Save normalized answer identities and report same-wrong-answer frequency, joint failures, vote margins, oracle coverage and correct-to-wrong versus wrong-to-correct aggregation transitions. Distinguish strict majority from plurality; arithmetic outputs often produce ties even with odd sample counts. Freeze invalid-answer handling, numerical equivalence and tie breaks.

Repeated homogeneous draws can be independent conditional on an item while being positively correlated across items through shared difficulty. Estimate within-item sampling behavior separately from across-item error overlap. Model lineage, shared training and similar prompts are hypotheses about covariance, not measured independence.

Pair comparisons by item; cluster uncertainty by arithmetic template if variants share construction. Reused model members and overlapping teams mean ensemble rows are not independent replications. Use item/template resampling and, if generalizing to a population of teams, team/member-aware uncertainty; report the number of unique items, models and rosters. Avoid treating all pairwise combinations as independent evidence.

Equal calls do not equal compute, and equal generated tokens do not equal total tokens. Record attempts, failures/retries, input tokens including transcripts, output and hidden reasoning tokens where measurable, model identity/version, latency and price assumptions. Equal ensemble size is the cleanest identity-diversity comparison; use separate token/cost frontiers when per-call compute cannot be equated. A vote parser is deterministic aggregation; a learned judge adds another model and inference budget.

## Baseline comparison gate
Before unblinding: preserve expectation file; request calibration/evaluation split, member accuracy uncertainty, frozen parser, tie rule, roster construction and token accounting. Compare homogeneous independent vote with heterogeneous independent vote first. Communication variants answer a second question. Strongest single member and budget-matched same-model self-consistency remain mandatory references. A positive mixed-model delta supports complementarity on this bank only; a null or negative delta does not show diversity is useless in general.
