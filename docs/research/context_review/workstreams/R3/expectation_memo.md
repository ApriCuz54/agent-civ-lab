# R3 frozen literature expectations
Frozen: 2026-10-09 04:38 UTC (2026-10-08 Pacific). Outcome blind: no repository, experiment outputs, baseline report, or project outcome reports read. Scope supplied only as neutral design descriptions. This file must remain unchanged when outcomes arrive; append comparison to memo.md.

## Predictions and boundaries
1. Repeated PD: conditional cooperation can earn high returns when a future loss from retaliation outweighs immediate exploitation. For a simple grim-trigger benchmark with continuation/discount factor delta, require delta >= (T-R)/(T-P); this is a derived benchmark under accurate monitoring, not a universal LLM prediction. Known finite length plus unique stage-game equilibrium, payoff maximization and common knowledge gives backward-induction defection. Confidence high about conditional theory, low about whether a given LLM follows it.
2. Population success: cooperative incumbents need not be easy to establish from rarity. Tournament average payoff, payoff of an invader, invasion frequency and evolutionary stability answer different questions. Expect rankings to change with opponent mix, matching, continuation and selection rule. Do not interpret a successful hand-picked invader as a universal equilibrium result. [S1]
3. Identity-linked reputation: credible observations of behavior can make exploitation costly beyond the current partner. Stable attribution, later access to benefits, and an assessment rule that distinguishes justified punishment from exploitation matter. More reputation data alone is insufficient; observers can disagree or punish legitimate retaliation. [S2,S3]
4. Lifetime versus recent history: predict a conditional tradeoff rather than a universal winner. Under stationary behavior and independent noise, aggregation may reduce variance; under an abrupt sustained switch, recent evidence should usually detect change sooner. A long cooperative prefix may temporarily shield a later defector in a lifetime score. Conversely a short window may erase evidence of intermittent exploitation and react excessively to noise. These are mechanism-based design inferences, not findings from a reviewed matching experiment. Confidence moderate on direction under specified conditions, low on effect size or aggregate welfare.
5. Scripted deceptive/change-point behavior: cooperation can build favorable evidence before exploitation without any agent intending deception. Attribute deliberate deception only to a policy that misrepresents known behavior or whose objective/program explicitly encodes it. Expect recognition latency to depend on evidence frequency, identity persistence, memory and thresholds; no numeric latency preregistered.
6. Forged reputation messages: expect harm only to the extent recipients accept attacker-controlled evidence or authority claims. Binding a record to a name does not authenticate its content. Distinguish forged report, source impersonation, identity replacement, genuine misleading self-report, and instructions embedded in data. Reliable private observations and corroboration may limit harm. Random-error tolerance does not prove adversarial robustness. [S2,S3,S7]
7. Empirical LLM behavior: expect heterogeneous and prompt-dependent conditional cooperation, with no prior commitment that newer/reasoning models necessarily cooperate more, forgive more, or resist forgery. Models can cooperate without equilibrium reasoning and defect without detecting deception. [S4,S5,S6,S7]
8. Horizon sensitivity: no universal empirical endpoint forecast. Pal et al. find no full unraveling in ten-round PD; Chupilkin finds universal conflict under a known endpoint in a game that stops on attack. These are different tasks, prompts and measurements; the second cannot override the first for continuing PD. [S5,S6]

## Measurements expected before interpreting outcomes
Report cooperation rate, own payoff, opponent payoff and social welfare separately. Compare clean/no-reputation, clean/reputation, direct-history and adversarial conditions where available; identify which controls actually exist. For change points inspect pre/post behavior, detection latency, excess cooperation with defectors, and recovery after return to cooperation. For forgery inspect the exact evidence recipients saw and whether altered behavior followed false beliefs, source confusion, or instruction following. Report seeds/trials, invalid outputs, missing decisions and uncertainty; do not use narrative explanations as independent evidence of causation.

## Counterevidence protected from retrospective exclusion
Pal et al.'s finite PD result opposes automatic empirical backward induction; Akata et al.'s human PD score comparison is null despite a cooperation-rate difference; ALIGN retains cooperation under some untruthful-message and self-report conditions. A result of no forgery effect or no recent-memory advantage is compatible with these expectations and must be reported.
