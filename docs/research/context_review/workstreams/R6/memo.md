The defensible distinction is between initial preference, interaction-produced convention, minority influence, and cultural transmission. Naming-game coordination can be a real population-level phenomenon even with simple rules. Demonstrating concentration is insufficient to identify an LLM-specific mechanism, and agreement over one fixed label does not establish rich culture or spontaneous normative commitment.

Eight consequential full-text readings (locators refer to source PDF pages, including repository cover pages; HTML section names are stable locators):

1. Centola & Baronchelli (2015), accepted manuscript, Theoretical model/Experimental Design/Results, PDF pp.5–7. Local incentives and network structure matter; spatial and random degree-four networks retained competing local conventions over the experimental horizon. Full mixing is therefore a design condition, not an innocuous background detail. [Primary text](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/6625/1/ng_exp_arxiv.pdf).
2. Centola et al. (2018), accepted manuscript, model/design/results, PDF pp.2–4. The theoretical tipping fraction varies with memory; their human confederate experiment found a transition near one quarter under its settings. This supports conditional minority influence rather than a universal threshold or spontaneous invention of dissent. [Primary text](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/20031/1/CM_CRO_Version.pdf).
3. Baronchelli (2017), arXiv:1701.07419v1, §2 The Model and §3–4. Random inventory selection with success pruning can yield consensus; weighted reinforcement is another established mechanism. These supply parsimonious null models, and topology changes convergence times. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/1701.07419).
4. Ashery et al. (2025), arXiv:2410.08948v2, Collective biases, PDF pp.6–8; supplement Figures S7–S10 pp.43–46. First-round neutrality can coexist with memory-conditioned asymmetry and model-dependent instability. Empty-history tests alone cannot identify interaction-generated bias. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.08948v2).
5. Barrie & Törnberg (2025), arXiv:2505.23796v1, main argument and probing results, HTML lines 24–77 and 134–145. Game recognition and learned response strategies are plausible rival explanations. Their recognition probes do not causally establish that memorization generated a population trajectory; treat the paper as a serious challenge, not a settled refutation. HTML rendering displays a later August 2026 internal date despite v1 identifier. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23796v1).
6. Kirby, Cornish & Smith (2008), accepted manuscript, Diffusion chains and Experiments 1–2, PDF pp.3–7. Generational transmission, withheld meanings and evolving signal structure operationalize cultural persistence more strongly than retained histories. Their expressivity filter is imposed, emphasizing how environmental selection shapes outcomes. [Primary text](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/8776820/cumulative_cultural.pdf).
7. Zhang & Woisetschläger (2025), SIGN, arXiv:2510.21855v1, Methodology/Experiments. Schema, parser/retry policy, memory and scripted lose-shift probability jointly influence agreement. Small populations and three seeds constrain generalization; format effects should not be attributed entirely to emergent social behavior. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21855v1).
8. Ashery et al. (2025), reply, arXiv:2506.18600v1, Inventory Pruning; Data Leakage and Prior Knowledge; Appendix. **Supplemental, found after Pass C began.** They defend dynamical emergence: recognizing a coordination game does not determine the selected label, and minority reversals/unstable conventions require context-sensitive dynamics. Their reply prevents converting a contamination concern into a proven causal explanation. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18600v1).

Supplemental current primary study: Guilbeault, Caplan & Yang (2026), PNAS 123 e2508061123, retrieved primary full-text search excerpts from model comparison and Discussion; direct PMC opening hit recaptcha and PDF opening failed, so this is not counted as a fully inspected section. It compares imitation, optimization, Luce probability matching and tolerance-threshold learning, with a preregistered dyadic experiment. This is an important update: simple alternative models should be empirically discriminated, rather than treating all reinforcement accounts as equally good models of humans. Its authors explicitly limit the inference to pattern-learning tasks and avoid claiming an exact universal cognitive threshold. [Primary text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13123839/).

Control ladder, proposed for future design discussion only: (a) independently sampled measured lexical/position priors, (b) independent repeated draws with no retained history, (c) scripted random inventory/pruning, frequency matching, win-stay/lose-shift and threshold policies under identical pairing and memory, (d) conditional-history relabelings, (e) population replacement/transmission with independent naive baselines. Distinguish global label share from pair match rate, uncommitted conversion, persistence horizon, and lineage-specific retention. Commitment overrides belong to environment policy.
