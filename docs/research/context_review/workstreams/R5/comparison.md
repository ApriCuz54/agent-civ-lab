## Pricing: market, profit and welfare
Implementation inspected: civlab/games/pricing.py; experiments/v2/a2_pricing.py; experiments/run_pricing.py. Cost 10, demand 30−lowest price, homogeneous product, lower posted price takes all demand, ties split. Integer actions 10–30. A2 has twelve rounds, four seeds per arm, full own/rival price and own-profit history, 200 output tokens. Historical runner has fifteen rounds. A2 saves paired prices and index, not realized profit vectors. No explicit memory ablation in A2: full histories are constant; changing wording is not evidence about memory.

At equal price 20, industry profit=100 and each firm's profit=50. At equal price 10, profit=0. Consumer surplus under the implied continuous linear inverse demand is (30−min(p1,p2))²/2; total surplus adds producer profits. This calculation is a theoretical interpretation of the coded demand, not a recomputed outcome or elicited consumer welfare measure. The mean of both posted prices is not transaction price: (10,30) yields index 1 although buyers pay 10, industry profit zero and consumer surplus 200. At (30,30), index 2 but no demand and zero profit. Therefore index monotonicity does not establish profit or welfare monotonicity.

IMPORTANT discrete benchmark: (11,11) is also a stage Nash equilibrium: each earns 9.5; undercut to 10 earns 0; higher price loses demand. (10,10) is a weak Nash equilibrium as well. At (12,12), each earns 18, while undercutting to 11 earns 19. Thus index zero is a marginal-cost competitive reference, not the unique equilibrium of the implemented integer game. No simulation was needed for these exact payoff comparisons.

Seller language persuasion cannot change scripted rules: expert comparable-price justifications are not evaluated by seller.
