# R5 expectation memo — Pass A
Frozen before repository/outcome access. Neutral designs supplied by coordinator: repeated LLM pricing with profit objectives and wording/memory variants; buyer bargaining against scripted seller with reservation values, outside options and deadlines.

Expected pricing behavior: wording and remembered history can change prices, but high prices alone do not establish tacit economic collusion. A valid claim requires a specified demand/cost game, competitive and joint-profit benchmarks, realized profits, sufficiently persistent supra-competitive outcomes, and preferably counterfactual deviation/punishment tests. Short finite horizons and truncation can produce apparent cooperation without incentive-compatible collusion. Index normalization is only meaningful if endpoints correspond to the implemented game.

Expected bargaining behavior: settlement price, deal rate, buyer surplus conditional/unconditional on agreement and outside-option-adjusted payoff can move in different directions. Deadline pressure should make concessions plausible but depends on seller policy. Scripted opponents test policy response rather than mutual strategic adaptation. Reservation-value violations, no-deal payoffs, hidden-information structure and seller acceptance rules matter more than linguistic aggressiveness.

Prior expectations: moderate prompt sensitivity; memory may stabilize or destabilize pricing. No universal prediction that more capable models collude more or bargain better. Null or sign-reversed effects are credible. Treat repeated calls within a trajectory as dependent, model revisions as distinct treatments, and few seeds as exploratory.
