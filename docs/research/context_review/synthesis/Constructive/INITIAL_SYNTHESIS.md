## Interpretation A: evidence interfaces can dominate behavioral advice

This is the strongest engineering interpretation if narrowed to controlled interfaces. Checked history can coexist with misleading annotation; explicit arithmetic/state/history assistance can outperform a generic moral instruction on a severe commons endpoint. Recent reputation and forged evidence suggest that record construction and integrity can matter more than a model's assumed cooperative disposition. The useful design question is which information channels to expose, authenticate and summarize for a specified objective.

Known before this package: unreliable-tool adoption, identity/authority cues, instruction conflicts, recency versus stale evidence, and explicit resource information all have nearby precedents. Frozen Coordinator:C-S1 TrustFork §§3.1–4,5.2–5.6, AppendixF and C-S2 Agents Overreliance §§3–5 already cover actionable subagent/tool reliance; R3:S2 cheap pseudonyms and R3:S3 assessment/indirect reciprocity cover identity/evidence dependence. These are author-extracted source claims pending verifier, not novelty certificates.

What this package adds provisionally: a reproducible cross-configuration panel linking checked temporal worker evidence, scripted self-report, refund wording, resource disclosure and failed game-derived repairs. Its potential distinctive contrast is added misleading annotation while independently checked evidence remains visible. It does not currently isolate the annotation's confidence, verification, length or attention components. Transparency is an arithmetic/history/equal-share bundle, and reputation forgery is a designed attack.

Why this framing could fail: the local effects may largely be objective specification or computation assistance rather than trust/evidence handling. It also risks assimilating dissimilar studies into a fashionable reliability narrative. A future clean channel-isolation design would be needed for a common mechanism; current evidence supports a narrow interface case report.
