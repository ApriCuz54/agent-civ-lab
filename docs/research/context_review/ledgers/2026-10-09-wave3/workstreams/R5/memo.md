## Evidence and design fit
S1 Fish, Gonczarowski and Shorrer, arXiv:2404.00806v2 (27 November 2024), §§2–5, Appendix B/D/E. Repeated differentiated Bertrand logit demand; 300 periods; 21 duopoly runs per prompt (seven per currency scale). Nash and joint-profit benchmarks, realized profits and off-path analysis support the authors' economic interpretation. P1/P2 change profit versus quantity emphasis; plans/insights are carried forward. Auctions include a noncollusive wording condition (§7). The relevant prior is prompt sensitivity and price-war concerns, not an exact protocol replication. Repository homogeneous integer prices, short horizon, explicit price-war wording and full numerical histories differ materially. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00806v2).

S2 Calvano et al., April 2019 working paper, FTC-hosted 42-page PDF, §§4.4–5.5, pp.19–29 of PDF. Q-learning in differentiated logit duopoly uses 1,000 independent sessions per parameter set; up to one billion iterations, convergence requires stable strategies for 100,000 periods. The representative parameter combination takes about 850,000 iterations. Deviation and punishment analysis is distinct from a high-price observation. This is a mechanism/comparator reference, not evidence about a twelve-round LLM trajectory. [Pinned working paper](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/public_events/1494697/calzolaricalvanodenicolopastorello.pdf). Published counterpart: AER 110(10), 2020, DOI 10.1257/aer.20190623; the inspected PDF is the earlier version, not silently substituted for final.

S3 Measuring Bargaining Abilities of LLMs, arXiv:2402.15813v2, §§3–4, Algorithm 1/Table 2 and Appendix H. 930 product scenarios, models against fixed ChatGPT gpt-3.5-turbo-1106; temperature zero; GPT-4 is gpt-4-0125-preview. Private buyer budgets/seller costs; payoff and deal/validity metrics; deterministic offer generator plus language narrator. Buyer difficulty and weak size improvements are contrary to universal capability optimism. Role and opponent selection affect conclusions. Its benchmark does not validate this project's three seller scripts. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15813v2).

S4 LLMs at the Bargaining Table, 2024 ICML Agentic Markets workshop, author-hosted 28-page PDF, §2 pp.5–6, §3 pp.7–9, §4 pp.11–13, Appendix B. Gains from trade is independent of transfer price; price bias is distribution; individual rationality is separate. The value/cost grid has 100 cells, ten conversations each; textual patience/information manipulations. Strong efficiency coexists with occasional irrational agreements. Prompt includes repeated hard reservation limits and strategy reminders. The inspected author PDF has no immutable version identifier: pin retrieval date and locator, and verify revision before definitive citation. [Author full text](https://www.renatoppl.com/papers/bargaining_llms.pdf).

S5 Liang and Xu, When LLM Agents Negotiate, arXiv:2608.07538v1, §3/Table 3, §4/Table 4, §6, Appendix D/Table 26. 9,840 negotiations across nine models; main symmetric block 9×16×15=2,160, plus role/provider and design extensions. Perfect Bayesian equilibrium comparator, explicit payoff/demand/prior/discount primitives. Distinguishes agreement, duration, undiscounted and discounted efficiency; high agreement can conceal costly delay and irrational contracts. Provider ordering is relational rather than universal capability ranking. This current preprint is stylized quantity/payment supply-chain bargaining, not laptop procurement field evidence. [Pinned full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07538v1).

S6 Calvano et al., Algorithmic collusion: Genuine or spurious?, IJIO 90 (2023) 102973, DOI 10.1016/j.ijindorg.2023.102973. Accessible abstract, introduction and conclusion distinguish memoryless/myopic high-price learning failures from forward-looking history-contingent strategies; exploration mode changes results. Author/institution full-text links failed. Treat this as screened contrary evidence, not a completed full-text assessment. [Publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167718723000541).

## Synthesis
Economic collusion is a strategy/incentive claim, not a synonym for high prices. Long-run profit, consumer surplus, competitive equilibrium and deviating against the observed policy are different measurements. Known-horizon backward-induction arguments require a unique relevant stage equilibrium and rational common knowledge; they cannot be applied mechanically here. Price-war wording already mentions mutual profit harm and can directly align objectives, so stronger language effects need not be autonomous discovery.

Bargaining advice can improve seller-specific exploitation without general strategic transfer. A buyer's normalized share of available surplus is not social welfare. Independent opponent policies, budget violations, no-deal outcomes and deadline responses should be reported separately. Current contrary evidence supports contextual, role-specific performance; nulls here remain failed superiority tests, not equivalence.

Novelty verdict: cross-model scope extension and constrained transfer evaluation. No discovery claim for prompt-sensitive pricing or LLM negotiation; no economic-collusion claim established by the index. Literature-to-repository causal mechanism transfer remains unproven.
