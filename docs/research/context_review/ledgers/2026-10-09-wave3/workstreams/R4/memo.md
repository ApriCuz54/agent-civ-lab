# R4 commons and institutions review — Pass A

Status: ready for baseline comparison after expectation_memo.md was frozen. No target-study results or repository were inspected. Six consequential full texts were inspected at the locators below; two additional candidates were unavailable. Source summaries and claims are deliberately concise. The 1991 Covenants working paper is not silently substituted for the paginated 1992 journal article.

## Evidence

**S1 Piatti et al., GovSim (2024), arXiv v3.** Sections 2.2–2.4, 3.4–3.7; Appendix D.3 Table 7. Simultaneous private extraction, public choices, subsequent discussion, doubling regeneration, and finite horizon make this the closest precedent. Universalization prompting improves aggregate survival, with model/scenario variation; Table 7 contains a zero survival-time change for Llama-2-13B. The intervention supplies a consequence/threshold cue, so improved outcomes do not isolate moral reasoning from arithmetic assistance or salience. The paper's subskill correlation is not causal identification. Keep finite-horizon survival separate from perpetual sustainability. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16698v3).

**S2 Ostrom, Walker, Gardner (1991 working paper).** Sections VI–VIII; PDF pages 35, 41–44 (one-based viewer pages; printed 34, 40–43), Table 10. Communication and self-selected sanctions support cooperation; costly sanctioning can erode net gains, and anonymous individual-decision information has a reported null effect (footnote 30). Institution adoption and sanction costs matter. This is evidence against identifying monitoring with effective enforcement. Published 1992 article located but not inspected. [Institutional full text](https://dlc.dlib.indiana.edu/dlcrest/api/core/bitstreams/0afb3404-76da-473c-ae70-b0f489e84624/content).

**S3 Dubois et al. (2020), PLOS ONE.** Materials and methods, Tables 1–2; Results, Treatment effect, Figure 2; Conclusion. Human fixed groups of four play twenty rounds with anonymous disclosure. Voluntary disclosure reduces extraction relative to mandatory disclosure; truthful versus freely chosen disclosure has a nonsignificant difference (p=.208). The absent aggregate-information-only arm limits claims about disclosure versus no disclosure. This is contrary evidence to a monotonic transparency hypothesis, not direct validation of LLM behavior. [Publisher full text](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0240212).

**S4 Ostrom (2010), Beyond Markets and States.** Published pages 652–656, especially updated design principles p.653 and laboratory experiments pp.654–655. Institutional success involves boundaries, locally appropriate rules, collective choice, accountable monitoring, graduated sanctions, dispute resolution, user rights, and nesting. These are contextual lessons, not a universal checklist. Communication and endogenous sanction choices differ from imposed regulation. A prompt telling agents to conserve tests only a narrow slice of this framework. [Full text university mirror](https://web.pdx.edu/~nwallace/EHP/OstromPolyGov.pdf).

**S5 Rehm (2026), GovSim-SelfGovern, preprint v1.** Sections 3.1, 4.2 Table 3, 4.3 Table 4, 6. Executable voted laws modify caps, penalties, redistribution, and membership. Governance helps standard-game intact survival; duress endpoints are unchanged and fatal-game gains are nonsignificant. This supplies a recent institutional null and a warning that commons survival can coexist with loss of members. Five seeds per model-cell and a restricted API limit generalization. The author explicitly disclaims causal identification of voting motivations. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22600v1).

**S6 Borah (2026), SovSim, preprint v1.** Sections 2.1–2.2, 3.2, Limitations. Changing decision order and extraction rights introduces asymmetric power and degrades survival relative to symmetric conditions across the tested models. These are structural manipulations, not mere labels or advice. The design omits richer communication/sanction mechanisms and holds four agents/twelve rounds fixed. Do not transfer effect magnitudes to symmetric target designs. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29062v1).

## Mechanism distinctions for Pass B

| Intervention | Immediate causal target | What remains unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| Resource/regeneration facts | uncertainty about feasible extraction | coordination, incentive conflict |
| Calculated equal sustainable share | arithmetic and numerical anchor | compliance, heterogeneity, fair division |
| Collective consequence instruction | salience and policy universalization | effects of numeric versus normative content |
| Expert advice | recommended action and authority cue | authority acceptance, enforcement |
| Other-user history | beliefs, descriptive norm, observability | imitation versus deterrence versus coordination |
| Inter-agent communication | common plans and negotiation | credibility and enforcement |
| Binding cap/fine/payoff rule | feasible actions or material consequences | legitimacy, costs, implementation quality |

The table is an analytical framework, not a verified description of target mechanisms. Read actual prompts and allocation code only during authorized Pass B. Advice may bundle authority, arithmetic, and consequence information; transparency may bundle social information, resource feedback, and salience. Without factorial contrasts, avoid attributing the combined treatment to a single mechanism.

## Limits and coverage

Recent primary preprints were searched through 2026-10-09; future-dated search hits were excluded. This is a focused review, not a systematic review. OpenReview's 2026 GovSim reproduction PDF was blocked by a browser-verification challenge at both attachment and PDF endpoints. Its search abstract was not used to assert reproduction findings. Nobel-hosted lecture PDF errored; the university-hosted published version was inspected instead. Governing the Commons (1990) book was not fully inspected; S4 provides the author's explicit updated formulation rather than pretending book-level coverage.

The neutral description of the second resource/other-user-information study was not enough to establish its bibliographic identity. No title or outcome was guessed. Baseline comparison should first establish operational equivalence: regeneration, stock observability, extraction versus request, oversubscription handling, history visibility, population, horizon, rewards, and estimate wording.
