The strongest directly relevant literature concerns confidence reporting and nonstationary evidence use. Human advice and automation studies offer mechanism analogies, not evidence that an LLM selector has human trust, social motives, or automation bias. None of the seven reviewed primary studies tests this exact selector design or externally unverified verification language.

## Consequential primary texts

**S1 Tian et al. (2023), Just Ask for Calibration.** Full PDF inspected at methods/results level, rather than abstract only. §2 pp.5434–5436 defines ECE, Brier score and selective accuracy/coverage AUC; Tables 1–5 pp.5435–5437 compare models and prompting. Numerical verbalization can outperform sampled conditional probabilities; benefits are inconsistent across models and metrics. ECE alone misses discriminative ability. §3 p.5437 reports no improvement from chain-of-thought calibration prompting. This contradicts a blanket claim that asserted numbers are necessarily meaningless. Generalization is limited by dated model versions, factual QA, model-based equivalence checking and sample-based probability estimates. [Full text](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.330.pdf).

**S2 Mielke et al. (2022), Reducing Conversational Agents' Overconfidence Through Linguistic Calibration.** Full PDF methods/results inspected. §3 p.859 separates expressed confidence from correctness; §§4–5 train a calibrator and controllable response generator. §5.4 and Figure 6 pp.866–868 show improved correctness among confidently worded responses, obtained partly by expressing confidence less often. Table 3 p.865 illustrates surface confidence edits. This supports separating language from underlying answer content and distinguishing calibration interventions from accuracy improvements. It does not establish that a second LLM will be persuaded by those edits. [Full text](https://aclanthology.org/2022.tacl-1.50.pdf).

**S3 Yang, Tsai, Yamada (2024), On Verbalized Confidence Scores for LLMs, arXiv v1.** Full HTML methods/results inspected. §3.2 separates calibration, informativeness and meaningfulness; §§4.3/5.4 and Figures 4–5 demonstrate model-size and prompt interactions. In §5.4 more complex methods can worsen small-model reliability while helping larger models; §5.2/Figure 3a finds confidence remains high as dataset difficulty rises. Contradiction between favorable and unfavorable confidence studies can therefore depend on elicitation protocol. Treat this as a versioned preprint, not a universal result. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14737v1).

**S4 Kim et al. (2024), I'm Not Sure, But…, arXiv v2.** Full HTML experimental design/results inspected. Preregistered N=404 human study. §3.4 holds substantive answer content constant while changing uncertainty wording; eight medical yes/no questions have half incorrect AI answers. Table 1 and §§4.1/4.5 show first-person uncertainty changes agreement and performance; general-perspective wording effects are weaker/non-significant. §5.1/Figure 2 conditions on AI correctness, with the reliance decomposition explicitly exploratory. Table 1 also has null differences on several perception/usage measures. These are human results only and support testing wording rather than assuming any confidence phrase has one stable effect. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.00623v2).

**S5 Zhou et al. (2024), Relying on the Unreliable, arXiv v1.** Full HTML model and human experiment methods/results inspected. §§4.2–4.3, Table 2 and Figure 5 describe 50-round human interaction with feedback, calibrated versus temporarily miscalibrated epistemic markers. Calibrated cues support learning; overconfident errors produce overreliance and lingering impaired use, while underconfidence produces underreliance. Plain answers can be treated like strong confidence. Important design limits: artificially controlled trivia task, 25 new participants per interactive setting and bottom-20% performance filtering (§4.2). This is an analogy to reputation/cue learning, not evidence of an LLM selector's inertia. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06730v1).

**S6 Cao et al. (2019), Nearly Optimal Adaptive Procedure with Change Detection for Piecewise-Stationary Bandit.** Full PDF formal setup, algorithms, assumptions and experiment sections inspected; proofs not independently verified. §3 pp.3 (PDF indexing from 1) defines selected-arm feedback and dynamic regret. §§3.3–4/Algorithms 1–2 pp.3–4 combine running-window change tests, UCB, uniform exploration and resets. §5 Assumption 1/Theorem 1 pp.4–5 requires adequate segment spacing and detectable change amplitudes. §4 emphasizes exploration because infrequently sampled arms conceal improvement. This supports drift-sensitive evidence use with caveats; it does not prove a recent-history prompt beats lifetime-history prompting. Its uncertainty bounds are algorithmic quantities, distinct from workers' self-asserted confidence. [Full text](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/cao19a/cao19a.pdf).

**S7 Goodyear et al. (2016), Advice Taking from Humans and Machines.** Full PMC methods and behavioral results inspected; neural findings are not used to infer LLM mechanisms. Materials and Methods / Subjects and Results / Behavioral Results, Figure 3, describe luggage screening with unreliable expert advice. Advice harms overall performance against a no-advice group. The overall main effect of human/machine Agent on advice utilization is null (p=.429), but an Advice×Time×Agent interaction occurs; late bad-advice reliance is lower with human than machine framing. This guards against treating human preference for automation as a universal main effect. Small samples and a specific false-alarm task limit transport. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5095979/).

## Synthesis and interpretation boundaries

Checked aggregate performance estimates source reliability; reported item confidence estimates a different target and could add information when calibrated. A selector's sensitivity to a manipulated confidence cue at fixed performance is evidence of cue influence; labeling it irrational additionally requires evidence the cue has no predictive or payoff value. A shift changes the estimand: lifetime accuracy estimates a mixture of past regimes, while a short window trades less stale-data bias for greater sampling variance. Exploration and feedback visibility determine whether either estimate can discover a changed worker.

Distinguish numeric probability, linguistic certainty, claimed verification, and observed verification. No reviewed primary text warrants collapsing them. Fixed-content wording manipulations are especially useful for inference, but changes in length, answer quality, worker identity or ordering remain possible confounds in a different design. “Automation bias,” “trust,” and “confidence heuristic” should remain human-literature analogy labels unless operationally defined for selector behavior.

The search deliberately retained favorable confidence evidence and null/heterogeneous human results. It was a targeted review, not an exhaustive systematic review or quantitative meta-analysis. Seven full-text sources were accessed and their consequential methods/results read; this is not a claim of cover-to-cover reading of every appendix. Search snippets/abstract-only candidates appear separately in sources.csv and support no substantive conclusion here. No paid APIs, experiments, secrets or publication were used.
