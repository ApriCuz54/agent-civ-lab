## Exact contrast implemented
analysis/everyday_effects.py:167–203 computes member matching accuracy from sample zero on the evaluated bank. For each anchor m, other configurations within ±0.05 are eligible; when fewer than four qualify, width expands to ±0.10; otherwise the anchor is excluded. It forms every four-member combination of the eligible pool. Each heterogeneous team consists of the anchor plus four distinct configurations, using only their sample-zero answers. Its per-item credit is averaged over all such teams; homogeneous credit uses all samples from the anchor. Difference is then averaged over items and eligible anchors. This is an anchor-weighted average over overlapping matched rosters, not seven independent mixed teams and not a comparison between optimally selected deployment ensembles.

vote_credit at lines 161–164 implements plurality with fractional expected credit among tied modal answers. Invalid answers share a single <invalid> vote label. The outcome is expected tie-broken accuracy rather than observed deterministic deployment accuracy. Both arms use the same rule, which is useful, but wrong-answer collision structure and invalid clustering still matter.

## Matching leakage and overlap
Same-bank matching is confirmed by both audit and code. The very sample-zero answers used to estimate capability also supply heterogeneous votes. Consequently matching conditions on evaluation outcomes; capability estimates are noisy and itemwise complementarity is unbalanced. The ±0.10 fallback is materially broad for 48 items. Matching is approximate per member, not equal mean ensemble capability nor held-out equivalence. It should be described as selected, approximately matched configurations on the observed bank.

Code intersects item IDs across the entire pool, not separately per four-member set. Missingness in any pool member can restrict every ensemble for the anchor; report metadata per anchor is needed to establish whether all 48 items remain. No rerun was undertaken.

All combination sets share the anchor and often other members. Averaging sets before bootstrapping avoids falsely counting each combination as an independent row. However hier_boot (lines 243–258) resamples anchor models and items separately within selected anchors; it does not preserve common item resampling across anchors, shared members, or matching-pool selection. Thus saved intervals are conditional on the fixed pool and do not fully represent roster/matching uncertainty. Their calibration under overlapping anchors is not established. Preserve historical interval but label the limitation; do not silently replace preregistered inference.
